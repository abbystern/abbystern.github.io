# Change 3: Editorial design and copy polish

## Goal

Make the portfolio feel more visually distinctive, sophisticated, and personal while reducing repetitive language across pages.

## Visual direction

Keep the site minimal and professional, but move it away from the feeling of a standard résumé/Jekyll template. Aim for an understated, chic editorial portfolio: intelligent, warm, modern, confident, and slightly unexpected.

The design should feel closer to a beautifully designed editorial or creative-professional site than a corporate landing page or tech startup website.

Achieve this primarily through:

- Stronger typography and typographic hierarchy.
- More intentional use of whitespace.
- A more striking homepage composition.
- Refined proportions and spacing.
- Subtle rules, borders, or small editorial details where useful.
- Thoughtful placement and treatment of the existing headshot.
- Restrained use of an accent color if the existing palette benefits from it.
- A strong contrast between display typography and highly readable body typography.

Prefer sophistication through restraint. Do not add gradients, stock imagery, illustrations, decorative icons, animations, excessive rounded cards, shadows everywhere, glassmorphism, or startup-style UI elements.

Keep the site responsive, accessible, single-column in spirit, and easy to navigate.

## Homepage

Make the homepage substantially more visually compelling and confident.

The homepage should introduce me, not summarize my entire résumé. It should create curiosity and give visitors clear paths to learn more.

Consider a more editorial hero treatment using my name, a concise introduction, my headshot, and the existing calls to action.

Do not make the hero look like a LinkedIn profile or résumé header.

## Copy

Review Home, About, Work Experience, and Contact together and identify ideas or phrases that are unnecessarily repeated.

Versions of the same concepts currently appear across the site: messy problems, people, systems, technology, operations, clarity, and making things work better. These ideas are genuinely part of my professional story, but repeating them on every page weakens them.

Give each page a distinct purpose:

- **Home:** A concise, memorable introduction. Create curiosity rather than explaining everything.
- **About:** The most personal and narrative page. Explain the progression from teaching to business school, strategy/operations, and technology. Preserve personality and warmth.
- **Work Experience:** Evidence. Let specific problems, actions, decisions, and results demonstrate how I work. Do not repeatedly explain my professional philosophy here.
- **Contact:** Extremely simple and warm. Do not summarize my background again.

Preserve factual information and the distinctions established in Change #1. Do not alter, combine, embellish, or invent any employers, roles, dates, achievements, metrics, or causal relationships.

## Writing voice

Keep the copy in my natural voice: direct, specific, conversational, thoughtful, confident, and human.

Prefer plain English. Mix concise explanatory sentences with occasional short declarative sentences. Let specific details carry the writing rather than describing me with adjectives.

Avoid generic AI/corporate language, including phrases such as:

- “at the intersection of”
- “passionate about”
- “uniquely positioned”
- “leveraging”
- “transformative”
- “dynamic”
- “in today's rapidly evolving...”
- Unnecessary “not just X, but Y” constructions.

Avoid excessive em dashes, rhetorical triples, overly neat conclusions, and copy that sounds optimized rather than written by a person.

Do not make every sentence punchy. Do not over-polish the personality out of the writing.

## Technical constraints

- Preserve the static Jekyll architecture and GitHub Pages compatibility.
- Do not add frameworks, libraries, tracking, a backend, a build system, or unnecessary JavaScript.
- Continue using Jekyll URL filters for internal links and assets.
- Preserve the résumé functionality added in Change #2.
- Maintain accessible contrast, semantic HTML, keyboard usability, and responsive behavior at approximately 375px and 1280px.

Do not implement anything yet. Create only `Change3.md`, show the proposed plan, and wait for approval before changing the website.
